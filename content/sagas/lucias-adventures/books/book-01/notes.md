## Core concept
Lucía uses a hand-drawn pictogram notebook to communicate when verbal language
feels overwhelming. This book introduces the notebook and shows its first real
social use.

## Anecdotes / real-life seeds

- The moment a child freezes when asked a question by a stranger — heart racing,
  perfectly capable of answering but unable to produce the words.
- The relief on a child's face when someone else "translates" without making them
  feel broken.
- The pride of adding a new card to a personal collection after a meaningful
  event. Like a trophy.

## Possible future scenes (sequel seeds)

- Lucía's notebook runs out of blank pages → she needs a new one → whole adventure
  around choosing it.
- Another child has their own different communication system (sign language,
  a tablet AAC app) → the two "books" meet.
- Lucía loses the notebook at the park → quest to find it → discovers she
  remembers more pictograms than she thought.
- School first day → notebook goes in the backpack → teacher learns about it.

## Character notes

- Mateo (the boy in this book) could return in a later book as an established
  friend, showing how the friendship developed.
- Leo's perspective story: a book from Leo's point of view about what it's like
  to be the "bridge" — and how sometimes he gets tired of it.

## Tone reminders

- Never frame Lucía's silence as a problem to be fixed. It is a trait, not a flaw.
- Other characters should react with patience and curiosity, not pity.
- Happy ending should feel earned, not forced.
