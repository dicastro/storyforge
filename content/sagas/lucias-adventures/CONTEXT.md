## Series overview

**Series title:** The Magic Notebook / El cuaderno mágico  
**Target audience:** Children aged 3–6 and their carers  
**Language:** Spanish primary, English secondary  
**Format:** Square picture book, 21×21 cm, 24 interior pages

The series follows **Lucía**, a 4-year-old girl who sometimes struggles to find
spoken words in social situations. She carries a small red ring-bound notebook
filled with hand-drawn pictogram cards, which she uses to communicate when speech
feels out of reach. Each book in the series places Lucía in a new social context
and shows — without drama — how she navigates it on her own terms.

The books are **not** about disability or therapy. They are about the naturalness
of different communication styles and the value of patience, creativity and
connection.

## Main characters

### Lucía (protagonist)
- Age: 4
- Appearance: messy light brown hair, turquoise t-shirt with small yellow pocket,
  red cloth wristband
- Personality: observant, empathetic, creative, selectively verbal
- Communication: uses her red ring-bound pictogram notebook when overwhelmed
- Key object: the red ring-bound notebook — always with her, growing over time

### Leo (Lucía's cousin)
- Age: ~5, slightly taller than Lucía
- Appearance: green t-shirt, short hair, light complexion
- Personality: cheerful, funny, perceptive, protective without being patronising
- Role: natural bridge between Lucía and new people — never takes agency away from her

## Tone and editorial rules

1. Lucía's silence is **never framed as a problem to be fixed**. It is a trait.
2. Other characters react with **patience and curiosity**, not pity or frustration.
3. Lucía always has **full agency** over her choices, including the choice to use
   or not use the notebook.
4. Leo helps but never speaks *for* Lucía in a way that diminishes her.
5. Resolutions feel **earned and quiet** — no forced happy endings, no magic fixes.
6. Language is simple, warm and rhythmical — suitable for reading aloud.
7. Short-text versions (one sentence per spread) must be complete and self-contained.

## Visual style anchor

All illustrations: children's book illustration style, soft textures, coloured
pencil and watercolour texture, warm and gentle lighting, cute and friendly
character design, clean background. **Strictly no text, no words, no letters in
any illustration.**

Character visual consistency is critical — see individual character files for
exact prompts.

## Series themes

- Communication diversity
- Inclusion and belonging

- Emotional intelligence
- Family support

---

## What each book should do

Each book in the series should:
- Introduce one clear social challenge for Lucía
- Show the notebook as a tool, not a crutch
- Include at least one moment where non-verbal communication is shown to be
  fully sufficient
- End with a small ritual that expands the notebook (a new card is added)
- Leave readers — children and adults alike — with a feeling of warmth and
  recognition, not instruction
